# Current-Law Alerts — October 2026

> [!warning] Exam-versus-law verification
> An older syllabus heading may remain examinable while the governing statute has changed. Document what the university expects **and** the current legal position. Ask your college for the latest course circular or recommended edition.

## BNS versus IPC
- KSLU circular dated **26 September 2024** introduced **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023** in Criminal Law I from academic year 2024–25.
- Use BNS statutory references for current answers. Old IPC papers should be mapped concept-by-concept, not copied with outdated section numbers.
- Primary text: https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/handle/123456789/20062
- Course circular: https://kslucorner.in/assets/syllabus/kslu-new-criminal-laws.pdf

## Consumer law
- KSLU's underlying 2018 Torts syllabus still says **Consumer Protection Act, 1986**.
- **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**, section 107, repealed the 1986 Act (subject to savings). Maintain a historic-to-current table.
- Reference: https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-107-88

## Labour law
- The older KSLU Labour Law I syllabus names the Trade Unions Act, Industrial Disputes Act, Payment of Wages Act, Factories Act and other prior laws.
- The Union government announced that four Labour Codes became effective **21 November 2025**; particular implementation and transitional issues require individual verification.
- Reference: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2192463&lang=1&reg=3
- Before exam use, cross-check the current KSLU paper syllabus and relevant commencement/rules.

## Article 370
- Constitutional Law II's older syllabus includes Article 370. Explain constitutional changes and current case law, not just its pre-2019 operation. Primary judgments must be checked before writing a definitive answer.

## Source hierarchy

1. Current official syllabus/circular and your paper's instructions (what will be examined)
2. India Code / Gazette / official legislative texts (what the law states)
3. Supreme Court or High Court full judgments and authoritative reports (what courts decided)
4. Standard prescribed textbooks (explanation and doctrine)
5. Study notes and AI drafts (only after verification)
