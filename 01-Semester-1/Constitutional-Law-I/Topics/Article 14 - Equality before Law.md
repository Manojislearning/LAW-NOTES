# Article 14 — Equality Before Law

## Issue / introduction

**Article 14 of the Constitution of India** prohibits the State from denying any person equality before the law or equal protection of the laws within India's territory. The two phrases have distinct historical emphases but work together to protect against unjustified unequal treatment.

## Essay structure

### 1. Constitutional text and scope

Explain **equality before the law** (absence of unjustified legal privilege) and **equal protection of the laws** (similar legal protection for similarly situated persons). The provision extends to **persons**, not just citizens.

### 2. Permissible classification

Article 14 does not forbid every legislative classification. In a conventional analysis of reasonable classification, discuss:

- An **intelligible differentia** distinguishing the grouped persons or things from others; and
- A **rational nexus** between the differentia and the law's legitimate objective.

### 3. Arbitrariness and substantive equality

Examine the doctrine that arbitrary State action may offend equality. For a complete answer, compare the older classification approach with later jurisprudence and explain how the question's facts trigger the relevant standards.

### 4. Application

Suppose a welfare rule provides assistance to one group of workers but excludes another. Identify the objective, the basis of distinction and whether the distinction reasonably connects to that objective; investigate arbitrariness and exceptions. Do not conclude without the statutory facts.

### 5. Cases to verify and add

- *State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar* — historical classification discourse.
- *E. P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu* — equality and arbitrariness.
- *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* — interaction of constitutional protections.

**Before exam use:** read the judgments or reliable law reports, capture precise citations and ratios. Case names alone are not a complete authority section.

## Short answer

Article 14 requires equality before law and equal protection of laws for every person. A reasonable classification generally needs intelligible differentia and rational nexus. Arbitrary State action also raises an Article 14 concern.
