# Constitutionalism

> [!warning] Study status
> Concept outline only. Case citations, doctrinal formulations and examination questions still require source verification.

**Unit:** [[Unit-01 - Constitution and Preamble]] · **Map:** [[08-Brain-Branches/Constitutional-Law-I/Unit-01 - Constitution and Preamble.canvas|Open Unit 1 Brain Branches]]

Constitutionalism is the principle that political power must be exercised under legal restraints, with institutions accountable to constitutional standards. A state can possess a written constitution without practising meaningful constitutionalism.

## Limited Government

Governmental authorities exercise powers conferred and limited by constitutional law. An exam answer should explain the difference between **a grant of power** and **a limitation upon power**.

## Rule of Law

Discuss government under law, accountability, non-arbitrariness and access to independent adjudication. Avoid treating the rule of law as merely a slogan; connect it to specific constitutional protections and institutions.

## Separation of Powers

Legislative, executive and judicial functions are distributed among institutions to avoid unchecked concentration of power. In India, describe the practical overlap and checks and balances rather than claiming an absolute separation.

## Judicial Review

Courts assess the constitutionality of legislation and executive action within their jurisdiction. Connect to [[Article 14 - Equality before Law]] and constitutional remedies studied later in the subject.

## Constitution versus Constitutionalism

**Constitution** asks: what is the foundational legal order? **Constitutionalism** asks: how is public power limited, justified and held accountable under that order?

## Exam-answer route

Definition → essential principles → relationship with rule of law, separation of powers and judicial review → Indian constitutional examples → conclusion.
